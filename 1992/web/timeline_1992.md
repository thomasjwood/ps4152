# 1992 campaign timeline (Gulf War to inauguration)

Each entry was checked against the source(s) linked at the end of its line (checked Oct 27, 2026).
Approval figures come from `bush_approval.csv` (Gallup via the American Presidency Project).
Poll figures come from `polls_1992.csv` and primary figures from `primary_results_1992.csv`.
"[W]" means the claim rests only on Wikipedia. Discrepancies between sources are flagged **NOTE**.

APP approval page: https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/statistics/data/presidential-job-approval

## 1990-1991: Bush's rise and fall

- **Jun 26, 1990** After a budget summit, Bush issues a statement saying a deficit deal needs "tax revenue increases," which breaks the 1988 "read my lips" pledge. The New York Post headline the next day: "Read my Lips: I Lied." [statement: https://www.presidency.ucsb.edu/documents/statement-the-federal-budget-negotiations ; headline: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Read_my_lips:_no_new_taxes]
- **Nov 5, 1990** Bush signs the Omnibus Budget Reconciliation Act of 1990, which raises the top income tax rate from 28% to 31%. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Read_my_lips:_no_new_taxes] [W]
  - Gallup approval falls from 67-76% in Aug-Sep 1990, during the Kuwait buildup, to 53-54% in mid/late Oct 1990. [APP]
- **Jul 1990 - Mar 1991** Recession. The NBER peak is July 1990 and the trough March 1991; FRED's USREC flags Aug 1990-Mar 1991. Unemployment keeps rising after the trough and peaks at 7.8% in June 1992. [FRED UNRATE/USREC: https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/USREC ; NBER: https://www.nber.org/research/data/us-business-cycle-expansions-and-contractions]
- **Jan 17 - Feb 28, 1991** Operation Desert Storm. The air war begins Jan 17 and the 100-hour ground war runs Feb 24-28. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War] [W]
  - Approval goes from 58% (Jan 3-6) and 64% (Jan 11-13) to 82% (Jan 17-20).
  - It peaks at **89%** approve, 8% disapprove, in Gallup's Feb 28-Mar 3, 1991 poll, the highest in the series. [APP]
- **1991** Leading Democrats stay out, among them Mario Cuomo and Al Gore (his son had been hit by a car). [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_United_States_presidential_election] [W]
- **Oct 3, 1991** Clinton announces his candidacy at the Old State House in Little Rock. [https://clintonschool.uasys.edu/?p=918 ; https://lrculturevulture.com/category/lr-look-back/page/14/]
- **Dec 10, 1991** Pat Buchanan launches his primary challenge to Bush in Concord, NH. The Dec 11 AP story says "Tuesday", which was Dec 10. He attacks Bush over the broken tax pledge. [https://nebnewspapers.unl.edu/lccn/sn96080312/1991-12-11/ed-1/seq-2.pdf]

## 1992: primaries

- **Jan 26, 1992** Bill and Hillary Clinton appear on *60 Minutes* after the Super Bowl to answer Gennifer Flowers's allegation of an affair. [https://csmonitor.com/1992/0128/28063.html ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Democratic_Party_presidential_primaries]
- **Feb 18** New Hampshire primary. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_New_Hampshire_Democratic_presidential_primary ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Republican_Party_presidential_primaries]
  - Democrats: Tsongas 33.2%, Clinton 24.8% ("the Comeback Kid"), Kerrey 11.1%, Harkin 10.2%, Brown 8.2%.
  - Republicans: Bush 53.2%, Buchanan 37.5%. **NOTE**: the outline's "Buchanan 37%" is right to the nearest point; Wikipedia gives 65,106 votes, 37.5%.
  - Bush approval: 39% in Gallup's Feb 19-20 poll. [APP]
- **Feb 20** Ross Perot announces his candidacy on CNN's *Larry King Live*, his fourth appearance on the show since 1991, and volunteer ballot-access drives start in every state. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Perot_1992_presidential_campaign] [W]
- **Mar 10** Super Tuesday. [primary_results_1992.csv, from https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Democratic_Party_presidential_primaries]
  - Clinton sweeps the South: TX 65.6%, FL 50.8%, TN 67.4%, LA 69.5%, MS 73.1%, OK 70.5%.
  - Tsongas wins MA (66.4%) and RI (52.9%).
  - Harkin quits the race around this date (LA Times, Mar 10: "Harkin Withdraws").
- **Mar 17** Clinton wins Illinois (51.7%) and Michigan (50.7%).
- **Mar 19** Tsongas suspends his campaign. [https://www.thecrimson.com/article/1992/4/10/tsongas-says-he-will-not-re-enter/ ; https://scholar.lib.vt.edu/VA-news/ROA-Times/issues/1992/rt9203/920320/03200159.htm] **NOTE**: Wikipedia's primaries article says he "left the race" on March 17. Contemporary reports give March 19.
- **Mar 26-29** Perot first appears in the polls, at 16% (NYT/CBS). By Mar 31-Apr 1 he has 24% (Gallup). [polls_1992.csv]
- **Apr 29 - May 3** Los Angeles riots, after the acquittal of the officers in the Rodney King beating. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Los_Angeles_riots] [W] **NOTE**: the same Wikipedia article gives both "55 people had been killed" and "a total of 63 people died". Use 63 with care, or say "more than 50".
- **Jun 2** California, New Jersey, Ohio and other primaries, the last big day of the primaries. Clinton takes California with 47.5% to Brown's 40.2%. [primary_results_1992.csv]
- **June: Perot leads three-way polls.**
  - Gallup Jun 4-8: Perot 39, Bush 31, Clinton 25.
  - Harris Jun 5-10: Perot 37, Bush 33, Clinton 25.
  - Gallup Jun 12-14: Perot 34, Bush 32, Clinton 24. [polls_1992.csv]
- **Jun 13** At Jesse Jackson's Rainbow Coalition meeting in Washington, Clinton condemns remarks by Sister Souljah. [https://m.csmonitor.com/1992/0625/25204.html ; https://fair.org/article/counterspin-no-8-june-26-1992] **NOTE**: Wikipedia only says "June 1992".
- **Jun 16** Independent Counsel Walsh indicts former Defense Secretary Caspar Weinberger. [https://irp.fas.org/offdocs/walsh/summpros.htm] **NOTE**: Wikipedia's Weinberger article says June 17.

## Conventions, Perot exits and returns

- **Jul 9** Clinton names Al Gore as his running mate. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Democratic_National_Convention] [W]
- **Jul 13-16** Democratic National Convention, Madison Square Garden, New York City. [same]
- **Jul 15** Ed Rollins resigns from the Perot campaign. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Perot_1992_presidential_campaign]
- **Jul 16** Perot withdraws, on the convention's last day, saying the Democratic Party had "revitalized" itself. He later claimed Republicans had planned to disrupt his daughter's wedding; the FBI found no evidence. [https://edition.cnn.com/ALLPOLITICS/1996/conventions/long.beach/perot/political.timeline.shtml ; https://pastdaily.com/july-16-1992-ross-perot-says-no-for-now/]
  - The two-way race that follows: Clinton 56-34 (Gallup Jul 17-18), 63-33 (Harris Jul 17-19). [polls_1992.csv]
- **Jul 31 - Aug 2** Bush's approval hits **29%**, his low in the series. [APP]
- **Aug 17-20** Republican National Convention, Astrodome, Houston. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Republican_National_Convention]
  - Aug 17: Buchanan's "culture war" speech: "There is a religious war going on in our country for the soul of America." [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_war_speech]
- **Sep 29** Judge dismisses the obstruction count against Weinberger. [https://irp.fas.org/offdocs/walsh/summpros.htm]
- **Oct 1** Perot re-enters the race. Polls just before had him at 7% (Gallup Sep 28-30); he finishes with 18.9%. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Perot_1992_presidential_campaign ; polls_1992.csv]

## Debates and the final weeks

Debate dates, places and viewership: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_United_States_presidential_debates [W]

- **Oct 11** First presidential debate, Washington University in St. Louis. Moderator Jim Lehrer; 62.4M viewers.
  - It was the first time three candidates shared a televised debate stage.
  - A CNN/USA Today poll of viewers called Perot the winner: Perot 47, Clinton 30, Bush 16.
- **Oct 13** Vice-presidential debate (Quayle, Gore, Stockdale), Georgia Tech, Atlanta. Moderator Hal Bruno; 51.2M viewers.
  - Stockdale opens: "Who am I? Why am I here?"
  - **NOTE**: Wikipedia calls the date "Thursday, October 13"; Oct 13, 1992 was a Tuesday.
- **Oct 15** Second debate, a town hall at the University of Richmond. Moderator Carole Simpson; 69.9M viewers.
  - Bush is caught checking his watch.
- **Oct 19** Third debate, Michigan State University, East Lansing. Moderator Jim Lehrer.
- **Oct 30** Walsh's second Weinberger indictment, one false-statement count, four days before the election. A Weinberger note in it puts Bush at the 1986 arms-deal discussions. [https://irp.fas.org/offdocs/walsh/summpros.htm ; https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Walsh]
  - Final Gallup poll, Oct 31-Nov 1: Clinton 44, Bush 36, Perot 14. [polls_1992.csv]

## Result and aftermath

- **Nov 3** Election Day. Source: FEC, *Federal Elections 92*, pp. 9-10: https://www.fec.gov/resources/cms-content/documents/federalelections92.pdf
  - Popular vote: Clinton 44,909,889 (43.01%), Bush 39,104,545 (37.45%), Perot 19,742,267 (18.91%), of 104,426,659 total.
  - Electoral votes: Clinton 370, Bush 168, Perot 0.
  - **NOTE**: Wikipedia's article gives Bush 39,104,550, Perot 19,743,821 and a total of 104,423,923.
- Perot's 18.9% is the highest share for a candidate outside the two major parties since 1912 (Theodore Roosevelt). He ran second in Maine and Utah. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_United_States_presidential_election] [W]
- Clinton's 43.0% is the second-lowest winning share of the 20th century, after Wilson in 1912. Bush's 37.4% is the lowest for a sitting president seeking re-election since Taft in 1912. [same] [W]
- **Turnout** [turnout_1980_2000.csv, from https://www.electproject.org/national-1789-present ; FEC p. 9]
  - VEP turnout: 58.1% in 1992, up from 52.8% in 1988; 51.7% in 1996.
  - FEC: 55.24% of the voting-age population (189,044,000) voted.
- **Dec 11** Weinberger's second indictment is dismissed. **Dec 24** Bush pardons Weinberger and several other Iran-Contra figures. [https://irp.fas.org/offdocs/walsh/summpros.htm]
- **Jan 8-11, 1993** Bush's last Gallup approval reading: 56%. [APP]
- **Jan 20, 1993** Clinton is inaugurated as the 42nd president. [https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_inauguration_of_Bill_Clinton] [W]
